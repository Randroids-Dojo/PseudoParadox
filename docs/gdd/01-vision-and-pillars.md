# Vision and Creative Pillars

**Status:** not_started

## Pitch

You wake up on the floor of a spaceship in a slightly rectangular room. You are disoriented. You do not know who you are or how you got there. Four doors line the walls. Each is a fixed portal to a different time in the same room. You escape by manipulating your own past instances, physically knocking them out, dragging their bodies, and coordinating their actions across generations of yourself.

Pseudo Paradox is a single-room time travel puzzle game. Every action is deterministic. No paradoxes are possible. The confusion is the puzzle.

## Creative Pillars

Prioritized, in order. Every design decision must serve a higher-numbered pillar before serving a lower-numbered one.

1. **Interaction with multiple selves.** The core mechanic. Everything serves this. If a feature does not deepen, clarify, or enable interaction between instances of the player, it is at best secondary.
2. **No paradoxes, ever.** Timelines are written and immutable. Player trust in the consistency of the world is non-negotiable. The system must never appear to contradict itself, even when it confuses the player.
3. **Sci-fi diegetic feel.** UI lives in the world wherever possible. The room tells the player what they need to know. One non-diegetic exception is allowed (active-instance floor ring) and is described in its own section.
4. **Logical puzzles.** Puzzles are natural extensions of the time-travel situation, not contrived obstacles. A puzzle that requires breaking the fiction to solve is a bug.

## What Pseudo Paradox is not

- Not a multiplayer game in v1.
- Not a multi-room game in v1. Single room only.
- Not a game where you prevent your past from happening. The fiction explicitly forbids this.
- Not a story-heavy narrative game. Narrative is implied by the room and by the player's own actions.
